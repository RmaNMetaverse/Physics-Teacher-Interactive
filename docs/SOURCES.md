# Sources and citation policy

Sources support the course's scientific claims, definitions, constants, and approximation limits. They do not replace original teaching explanations or grant permission to copy prose, figures, examples, or assessments.

## Selection policy

Prefer, in order:

1. standards bodies and measurement institutes for SI definitions and constants;
2. peer-reviewed literature or authoritative institutional technical material for scientific claims;
3. established, openly accessible university-level textbooks for foundational exposition;
4. specialist references when a lesson's scope requires them.

Use a direct durable URL rather than a search result. A lesson reference needs a descriptive label, working HTTPS URL, and a `reviewedAt` date. Cite the source closest to the claim. Cross-check surprising quantitative claims with an independent authoritative source. Do not cite generated text, unsourced aggregators, or a page merely because it repeats the desired formula.

Explain content in original words and derive worked examples independently. Quote only when exact wording is essential, keep it short, mark it as a quotation, and follow the source license. Record attribution for any separately licensed media in the repository alongside the asset.

## Foundation references

These are useful starting points; individual lessons carry the references actually consulted.

- Bureau International des Poids et Mesures, [The International System of Units (SI Brochure)](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure).
- US National Institute of Standards and Technology, [SI Units](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Units/).
- OpenStax, [University Physics Volume 1](https://openstax.org/details/books/university-physics-volume-1), for mechanics and oscillations.
- OpenStax, [Physics](https://openstax.org/details/books/physics), for algebra-based foundational treatments.

Open-access status does not remove attribution or license obligations. Check the current license on the source itself before adapting any material.

## Maintenance

When changing a scientific explanation, formula, parameter range, or limitation, re-open the cited source, update broken or superseded links, set the lesson's review date to the actual review date, and note the scientific change in `CHANGELOG.md`. The repository-wide editorial cutoff is context only; each lesson's source list is the auditable record.
